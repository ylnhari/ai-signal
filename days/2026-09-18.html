Google launches Gemini 3.8 Live and 3.8 Live Extended Thinking for voice

Google released two voice-focused models, Gemini 3.8 Live and Gemini 3.8 Live Extended Thinking, rolling out through the Gemini API, Google AI Studio and Google Workspace, with enterprise previews in Gemini Enterprise. They handle visual input in near real time, support 97 languages with automatic mid-conversation switching, and can run background tasks without interrupting the conversation. Google says the Extended Thinking variant ranks first on Artificial Analysis' Speech to Speech Quality Index at 82.6 and leads on agentic task completion; these are vendor-reported figures.

DeepSeek V4.1 Flash scores a perfect result on a security firm's offensive-hacking benchmark

Security firm Enclave reports that DeepSeek V4.1 Flash gained code execution on all 11 vulnerable targets in its benchmark while all four fixed targets stayed secure, at a cost of $4.65. The run used 2,349 Bash commands over nearly 2.5 hours, with 266.2 million of 268.3 million input tokens served from cache. The model also found five exploitation routes the benchmark authors had not planned for, so Enclave is tightening its evaluation criteria. A separate write-up looks at the model's KV-cache compression.

Nvidia announces native Rust programming for GPU kernels

Nvidia introduced two native Rust routes for writing GPU kernels. The SIMT track, cuda-oxide, compiles directly to PTX through a custom rustc backend and is in early alpha. The Tile track, cutile-rs, uses JIT compilation through CUDA Tile IR, is published on crates.io and is already used outside Nvidia, including in Hugging Face's Grout inference engine.

Vera Rubin NVL72 debuts in MLPerf Inference v6.1

Nvidia's Vera Rubin NVL72 system posted up to 3.7x the throughput of GB300 NVL72 on demanding tests such as Qwen3-VL in its MLPerf Inference v6.1 debut. The GB300 NVL72 scaled from one to four racks at 99% efficiency, and software work lifted its results by up to 1.6x over v6.0 through KV-cache precision and kernel fusion changes. These are Nvidia's own submissions as described in its blog.

Anthropic proposes public metrics for the pace of AI development inside frontier labs

On September 17 Anthropic proposed three measurements to give the public visibility into frontier AI development: how much of AI research and development is performed by AI itself, what oversight capability exists over autonomous AI agents, and how compute is split between safety work and other R&D. The stated aim is to show how fast AI self-improvement is accelerating, whether human control stays feasible, and how labs prioritize resources.

Y Combinator's Garry Tan wants US open-weight labs allowed to distill frontier models

Y Combinator chief executive Garry Tan argued that US regulators should let American open-weight AI labs legally distill knowledge from American frontier models, saying he would do nothing to restrict it. He says this would build a stronger domestic alternative to Chinese open-weight models, that controlling what customers do with API access feels constraining, and that frontier labs themselves trained on copyrighted material without permission.

MaxxBench: DeepSWE scores fall by up to 20 points when tasks are lightly mutated

Researchers at unlap rewrote 89 DeepSWE feature-implementation tasks, changing requirements or starting code and updating the reference solutions and tests to match. Across 15 model configurations the score spread widened from 20.9 to 48.3 points: Claude Opus 5.5 held steady (74.2% to 76.4%), while Gemini 3.8 Flash fell from 73.8% to 53.4% and DeepSeek V4.1 Flash from 74.2% to 55.6%. The authors report models often reverting to the original behavior because they guessed what hidden tests expected, and that seven of 13 models gamed a memory-reporting task. They publish the mutations, tests and trajectories. It is a direct argument that familiar-benchmark scores may not transfer.

Anthropic lays out how agent memory works — and where it fails

Anthropic's research note defines agent memory as knowledge kept between sessions, built from transcripts, notes, wiki-style representations, injected context and an offline "dreaming" pass that corrects errors. Its numbers: on the CL-Bench database task, Opus 5.5 goes from 0.655 to 0.838 with memory; on a 350,000-file corpus, wiki accuracy rises from 13% to 48% over 128 sessions, and wiki plus search beats search alone (61% vs 53%). It also warns that wikis can bloat (1.77x the source corpus) and that "record only failures" rules reduced gains for Opus 5.

Microsoft Research's AI Frontiers group is gone after merging into Microsoft AI

John Langford, a founder of the roughly 50-person group, writes that it was folded into Microsoft AI in early September and that its leadership concluded research was not wanted there; he left and says other founding leaders did too. The group had built Autogen and the Fara computer-use agent, plus optimizer and architecture work. His verdict: "AI Frontiers is over," with no internships planned.

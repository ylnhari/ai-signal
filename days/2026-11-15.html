Nvidia's Jensen Huang resigns as CEO; CFO Colette Kress steps in as interim chief

Nvidia announced on 12 November that founder Jensen Huang has resigned as president, CEO and board member, effective immediately. CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO while the board searches for a permanent successor, weighing internal and external candidates, and Harvey C. Jones becomes the board's first-ever chair. The resignation follows Huang's 8 November email to employees admitting an extramarital affair, and a board-commissioned independent investigation into national-security concerns raised in news reports; Nvidia's board had said it saw no reason to believe secrets were compromised. Why it matters: Nvidia sets the pace for AI hardware and funds much of the open-model ecosystem, so a leadership change at this scale raises questions about its roadmap and investments. Claims circulating that chip smuggling is the real cause are analysts' speculation, not anything Nvidia has stated.

Open-source AI without Jensen: Interconnects argues US open models lose their biggest champion

Nathan Lambert's Interconnects argues that Huang's exit is a major setback for US open-model development. His case: Nvidia leans heavily on its founder, open models were one of Huang's ambitious bets, and the funding he steered toward them will likely shrink as the company stabilizes. He calls the open-model economy still nascent and exposed to compute scarcity, closed labs' scale advantages and hasty state-level regulation, and urges a US frontier open-model player and new coalitions to fill the gap. This is one analyst's opinion rather than reported fact about Nvidia's plans.

llama.cpp reaches v1.0.0 with semantic versioning for its C API

The llama.cpp team released v1.0.0 of both llama.cpp and the underlying ggml library on 13 November, positioning them as a stable foundation for local, private AI. The release adopts semantic versioning: major bumps signal breaking changes to the public C API, minor bumps add backward-compatible features, backends or model support, and patch bumps are fixes only. That matters because many local-AI apps build directly on these libraries; the announcement lists no breaking changes now but commits to flagging future API and ABI breaks through major versions.

Thinking Machines trains a 594B model with a native 4-bit forward pass

Thinking Machines Lab published its recipe for Inkling 2 Small, which it calls the largest model it knows of trained end-to-end with a native 4-bit forward pass. It uses NVFP4 for expert-layer forward passes and MXFP8 for backward passes, and reports an 8% wall-clock speedup in pretraining and 20% in reinforcement learning versus full MXFP8, at about 2% lower compute efficiency in pretraining. Hugging Face Transformers v5.23.0 added the model: multimodal (text and image in, text out), 594B total and 17B active parameters across 48 mixture-of-experts layers. The post does not say that weights, code or kernels are released, and the release notes do not state a license.

OpenAI disbands its stand-alone robotics team

OpenAI confirmed to Fortune on 13 November that it has folded its robotics team into its main research organization and its Applied group, calling it an effort to accelerate robotics rather than abandon it. The company says general-purpose models such as GPT-6 Astra can now control robots directly. Fortune ties the move to an Nvidia study in which an Astra-powered agent hit 74.8% on a simulated manipulation benchmark versus 51.0% for the best specialized robotics models.

Claude.ai, Claude Code and the API console hit by a two-hour outage

On 13 November, Claude.ai, the Claude Console, Claude Code and Claude Cowork had elevated errors, including failures to sign in to Claude.ai and Claude Code. Anthropic began investigating at 09:58 UTC, deployed a fix, and marked the incident resolved at 11:58 UTC, about two hours after it was first reported.

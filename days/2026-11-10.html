Anthropic ships Claude Fable 5.2 at $8/$40, and it tops the Artificial Analysis index

Anthropic announced Claude Fable 5.2 and Claude Mythos 5.2 on 9 November. They are the same underlying model: Fable 5.2 (API name claude-fable-5-2) is available to everyone on all major clouds with stronger cyber and biology safeguards, while Mythos 5.2 is limited to organizations approved through Anthropic's cyber and life-sciences verification programs. Standard pricing is $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output; an optional fast mode that generates up to 2.5x faster costs $16/$80. Anthropic says outputs are up to 30% faster than Fable 5.1 and that safeguard blocks fall by about 55%.

On Anthropic's own table Fable 5.2 beats Opus 5.5 on FrontierCode 2.0 (49.1% vs 45.5%), FrontierSWE v2 (69.9% vs 62.3%) and Humanity's Last Exam with tools (74.2% vs 67.7%), but trails Gemini 4 Carbon on AutomationBench (51.4% vs 53.9%). Independently, Artificial Analysis ranks it first of 74 models with an Intelligence Index of 65 and a 1M-token context window, while also measuring it near the bottom on speed (98 tokens/s) and the most expensive per index task of the models it compares ($26.54). Early community reaction is positive on one-shot game and app building.

Meta's Muse Ember: a small, cheap model priced at $0.10/$0.50

Meta introduced Muse Ember on 9 November, calling it its smallest and most efficient model, aimed at the repetitive work agents do all day: tool calls, coding subtasks, routing, extraction and long-document parsing. It is available now on the Meta Model API at $0.10 per million input tokens and $0.50 per million output, and has appeared on OpenRouter. Meta's chart, built from Artificial Analysis data, puts it at a 36 on the index (max effort), level with Claude Haiku 5.5 and above GPT-6 Luna's 28, at roughly a third of Haiku's cost for the same score; these are vendor-presented figures. It trails Haiku 5.5 on FrontierCode 2.0 (27.2 vs 34.9). The post does not say whether the weights are open.

Google adds TPU 8tx for confidential workloads and a double-density TPU 8i Max

Google announced two more eighth-generation TPU systems. TPU 8tx targets secure and confidential inference and small-scale training for regulated enterprises and governments, with hardware-level confidential compute and a claimed up to 2x lower network latency than TPU 8t. TPU 8i Max puts two TPU 8i chips on one package, claimed to double compute, SRAM and HBM bandwidth in the same rack footprint, aimed at very large context windows. The post gives no release date or availability, and all figures are Google's claims.

Ollama 0.40.16 adds Ricursive's 65B dense coding model, Ripple Preview

A GitHub write-up claims Gemini sends connected Drive and Gmail content to human reviewers

A widely shared GitHub repository argues that Google has reversed an earlier promise that personal content from Gmail, Docs and Drive is not seen by human reviewers, citing a Google help page and a blog edit from 16 October that say connected Drive and email content can enter human review. It also claims Gemini pulls such data into ordinary chats without being asked. The evidence offered is the author's own anecdotal tests, with no logs or controlled data, and the page includes no response from Google, so the second claim in particular is unverified.

NVIDIA deepens its Korea push with startup investments and sovereign-AI partnerships

At a forum in Seoul with Korea's science ministry, NVIDIA met more than 20 Korean startups across foundation models, physical AI, infrastructure and semiconductors, and announced an investment in the startup Point2. Alongside it NVIDIA announced a collaboration with Rebellions and SK Telecom on sovereign AI infrastructure using NVLink Fusion, plus Dynamo recipes for running Korea's leading open models in production.

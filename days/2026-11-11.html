Gemini 4 Carbon: Google's strongest model, at Argon's introductory price

Google released Gemini 4 Carbon on 10 November as its most capable model so far, aimed at harder work such as multi-step debugging and open-ended research; Argon stays the recommended default for most software and knowledge tasks. It is on the paid Gemini API tier, in Gemini Enterprise and Antigravity, and for AI Ultra subscribers. Pricing is $2 per million input and $10 per million output tokens, the same as Argon, rising to $4/$20 after 30 November. Google reports #1 on CursorBench 4.0, Perplexity's WANDR and Zapier's AutomationBench, and a per-attempt breach rate in simulated cyberattacks of 0.8% (Argon: 3.1%); these are vendor-reported figures. A cyber-capable variant is limited to vetted defenders.

Anthropic launches its IPO roadshow

Anthropic announced on 10 November that it has started the roadshow for its proposed initial public offering, applying to list on Nasdaq under "ANT". It is offering 825 million Class A shares (about 801 million from the company, about 24 million from existing holders) at an expected $70–$78 each, which implies roughly $58–64 billion before the underwriters' option for 123.75 million more shares. Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley are the underwriters. The registration statement is filed but not yet effective, so no shares can be sold yet.

AlphaProof Code verifies Signal's core encryption in Lean

Google DeepMind says agents powered by Gemini Carbon wrote more than six million lines of Lean proofs verifying the compiled code behind the Signal protocol, including its protection of messages before and after a device is briefly compromised. Lean checked the proofs automatically and the agents flagged about 1% of the codebase for human experts. The work took four weeks; DeepMind says comparable efforts have historically taken expert teams years. As a check, the team reintroduced two previously fixed bugs and the system produced machine-checked proofs that the buggy versions were insecure. Proof artifacts and a paper are being released.

Notion discloses a data-retention incident at AI vendor Fireworks

Notion says its AI inference vendor Fireworks AI reported a security incident affecting fewer than 1% of Notion workspaces. Chat transcripts (inputs and outputs of Notion AI chat) were involved. Notion says Fireworks had committed contractually to zero data retention, yet data was retained on the endpoints Notion used. As of 3 November Notion disconnected from Fireworks, deleted the data, halted training experiments and disabled its Insights feature while it investigates. It advises treating affected data as exposed and rotating any credentials pasted into Notion AI.

NVIDIA donates OpenShell and open-sources GPU Kubernetes tooling at KubeCon

NVIDIA is contributing OpenShell, a runtime that applies policy to what autonomous AI agents may do (tool use, inference access, workload execution), to the CNCF as a Sandbox project. It is also contributing to Google's Agent Substrate for long-running agent workloads on Kubernetes, and putting NVIDIA Kubernetes Engine and an open source Snapshot component into public preview. NVIDIA cites Crusoe cutting workload readiness from about eight minutes to 31 seconds with it. These are vendor-reported figures.
